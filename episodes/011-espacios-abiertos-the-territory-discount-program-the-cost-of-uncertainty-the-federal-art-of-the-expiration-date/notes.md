Medicaid comes in two versions. In the 50 states and D.C., Washington matches
whatever qualifying care a state spends, between **50 and 83 cents** on the
dollar depending on per-capita income, with no annual ceiling. In Puerto Rico
the same program comes with a fixed match written into the Social Security Act
and a Section 1108 annual allotment, and once the allotment is spent every
additional dollar of care is **100% local**. This episode reads *El costo de la
incertidumbre: Medicaid en Puerto Rico y las brechas en financiamiento
federal*, the September 2026 paper by Espacios Abiertos (author Angélica V.
Marrero Sánchez), and follows the clock Congress set in the Consolidated
Appropriations Act of 2023: a federal art of the expiration date, applied to
1.3 million people.

In this episode:

- **One program, two versions.** The states get an open-ended match. Puerto
  Rico gets a "territory discount": a match that Congress sets by hand and a
  cap that ends the federal share for the year. CMS says so on its own Puerto
  Rico page; CRS reports R47601 and IF11012 restate it.
- **The clock now running.** P.L. 117-328 wrote five caps into law,
  **$3.275B, $3.325B, $3.475B, $3.645B and $3.825B** for FY2023 to FY2027; set
  Puerto Rico's match at **76% through September 30, 2027** while making
  **83% permanent** for American Samoa, CNMI, Guam and the U.S. Virgin
  Islands; and ordered FY2028 to be rebuilt from the **FY2019 allotment** plus
  medical inflation, ignoring everything appropriated since. Add the
  conditional **$300 million** (physician-rate floor) and **$75 million**
  (program integrity) and FY2027 is worth about **$4.2 billion**.
- **The FY2022 warning shot.** The last time the formula pointed back at
  FY2019, HHS priced the result at **$406.4 million**. CMS used a FY2020 base
  and paid **$2.943 billion** instead; GAO ruled that unauthorized. Congress
  answered by writing the FY2019 base into the FY2028 instruction so the fix
  cannot be repeated. The 2027 cliff is not a rumor; it is the statute
  repeating the math on purpose.
- **Three scenarios, one spend path.** Holding coverage constant, the island
  spends about **$5.32 billion** on Medicaid in FY2028. Scenario 1, the law
  as written: match falls to **55%**, the cap resets to about **$471 million**,
  and the local bill is **$4,849 million**, **35.4%** of the General Fund.
  Scenario 2, extend and inflate the cap: federal **$3,924 million**, local
  **$1,396 million**, and a gap that widens every year because the cap grows
  at 2.6% while costs grow near 5%. Scenario 3, the Governor's May 2026 ask:
  **$4,415 million at 83%**, local **$905 million**. The cap, not only the
  match, is the cliff: at 55% with no cap the federal share would still be
  about $2.9 billion.
- **Who is on the program.** **1,346,659** enrollees as of August 26, 2026
  (25% under 21, 27% over 60), roughly four in ten residents; about
  **292,000** dual eligibles on Medicare Platino; community health centers
  drawing about 70% of revenue from Medicaid and serving some 300,000
  patients.
- **Still not a state-like program.** Home health, non-emergency transport,
  durable medical equipment and hospice only began in **July 2024**, at a
  forward cost above **$500 million** a year, already larger than the
  modeled FY2028 cap. The eligibility line moved toward federal poverty
  parity in 2023 for about **$29 million** a year. Long-term nursing-facility
  care is still missing.
- **The second bill.** Law 72-1993 municipal contributions of about
  **$164 million** were reduced while the match exceeds 55%. At 55% the
  municipal bill comes back, on top of the General Fund.
- **What "700,000 to 1 million" is and is not.** Espacios Abiertos did not
  model disenrollment; the range is a government and hospital-sector warning
  it repeated. A loss that size would need a policy choice: tighten the
  poverty line, drop the 2024 benefits, or cut plan rates until carriers
  exit.
- **What to ask for.** Delete the cap and let the state formula set the
  match, which would give Puerto Rico the 83% maximum. If Congress will only
  extend: at least five years, a cap indexed to actual Medicaid cost growth,
  and an FY2028 base that does not snap back to 2019. Raising the match
  without lifting the cap only empties the cap faster.

The closing question: Congress wrote the expiration date. House Energy and
Commerce said in mid-September 2026 that a solution had not been found. The
island's contingency plan for October 1, 2027 does not exist on paper yet.
Which of the two gets written first?
