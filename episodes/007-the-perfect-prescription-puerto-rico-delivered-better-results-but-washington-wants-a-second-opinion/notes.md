Puerto Rico runs one of the most efficient pharmacy systems in the country - and Washington still treats it like an exception instead of a model.

In Medicare Advantage, federal funding follows the star ratings, and the stars are driven by medication adherence: are patients actually taking their prescriptions for chronic conditions like hypertension, diabetes, and high cholesterol? In 2013, the Asociación de Farmacias de Comunidad de Puerto Rico (AFCPR), under the presidency of Elliot Pacheco, built a unified adherence program and took it straight to the Medicare Advantage plans - shifting community pharmacies from simply dispensing pills to actively managing patient care (medication synchronization, reconciliation, interaction checks). Think of it as a racing pit crew tuning the engine, not just handing the driver spare parts.

The federal data from 2020-2023 tells the story:

- Ranked #1 of all 56 U.S. jurisdictions for both highest utilization and lowest gross drug costs
- ~19% more prescriptions filled per year than the national benchmark
- ~30% less per fill - about $63 vs. $89 nationally
- A deliberate, generics-first culture of high adherence for chronic conditions

The paradox: despite delivering five-star performance, Puerto Rico is statutorily boxed out of the low-income subsidies residents of the 50 states receive automatically - an estimated $250 million in foregone assistance each year, and up to $2 billion under full parity. If it runs this well on empty, imagine what it could do with a full tank.
