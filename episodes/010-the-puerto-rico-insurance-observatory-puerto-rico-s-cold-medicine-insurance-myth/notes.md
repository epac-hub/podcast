Put **$440** on the kitchen table. That is what Puerto Rico's healthcare system
has, per person, per month, to buy a full month of comprehensive coverage for
everyone: the neighbor with diabetes and kidney failure, the kid with severe
asthma, the ICU stay, the oncology regimen. On the mainland, $440 barely buys
a catastrophic plan with a five-figure deductible. Out of that gap grew the
island's most quoted cynical line, attributed to Dr. Víctor Ramos: *planes que
solo cubren medicinas de catarro*, health plans that only cover cold medicine.
This episode opens the [Puerto Rico Insurance Observatory](https://insurancepr.org) and reads the sworn,
audited **Planillas de Salud** filed with the Office of the Commissioner of
Insurance (OCS) and the NAIC to test that myth against the numbers. No
politics; only what the filings say.

In this episode:

- **The $440 problem.** In 2025 the market-wide filings show about
  **$17.18 billion** in written premium (the Core 9 statutory entities alone
  report **$17.05 billion**, up from $15.33 billion in 2024) covering
  **2,999,355** lives, virtually the whole island. Divide it out and you get
  roughly **$5,325** a year, or **$440** a month, per person, to carry
  hospitals, specialists, pharmacy and the insurers' own overhead.
- **An inverted pyramid.** **61.9%** of covered lives depend on federally
  funded programs: Plan Vital (Medicaid) **41.4%**, Medicare Platino **11%**,
  Medicare Advantage **9.1%**, Medicare Supplement 0.4%. The money does not
  follow the headcount: Platino brings in about **$1,443** per member per
  month and standard Medicare Advantage **$996**, while Plan Vital runs on
  **$336** and private individual plans on **$214**. The base survives only on
  volume.
- **Medicare Advantage pays the bills.** Traditional fee-for-service Medicare
  is statistically negligible on the island; almost every senior is in a
  privatized Medicare Advantage or Platino plan (Platino members are dual
  eligibles managed as Advantage patients). In 2025 Platino was **33.4%** of
  all premium (**$5.73 billion**) and standard Advantage **19.0%**
  (**$3.27 billion**): together **over 52%** of every healthcare dollar, about
  **$9 billion**. Hospitals, specialist networks and labs live on those
  reimbursement rates and cross-subsidize the Medicaid and commercial
  populations with them.
- **A game of titans.** The market is concentrated in a handful of carriers:
  MCS Advantage (about **$4.73 billion** in premium, up **31.2%** in a year),
  MMM Healthcare (**$3.29 billion**, 214,969 covered lives), Triple S, First
  Medical (**$1.62 billion**, dominant in Plan Vital), Humana and Plan de
  Salud Menonita. Each picks a lane, Medicaid volume or Medicare complexity,
  because at $440 a month scale is not a strategy, it is the price of
  admission.
- **Billions in premium, nothing left over.** The Core 9 statutory entities
  closed 2025 with a combined net loss of **-$19.8 million**; only five of nine
  were profitable. The best result, MCS Advantage at **+$101.3 million**, is a
  **2.1%** margin on $4.73 billion. Triple S Advantage lost **-$86.6 million**,
  Humana Health Plans of Puerto Rico **-$63.2 million**, MMM Healthcare
  **-$8.9 million**. First Medical swung from a near -$40 million loss in 2024
  to +$5.3 million in 2025. Why: IBNR estimates (claims incurred but not yet
  billed) that miss by a point or two, and administrative ratios that range
  from a lean **6.9%** (Triple S Salud) and **8.7%** (MCS Advantage) to
  **17.2%** (Humana) when the hospitals need 85 to 90 cents of every dollar.
- **How they stay standing.** Losses are absorbed from capital and surplus
  (*capital y excedente*: admitted assets minus total liabilities), parent
  company injections and reinsurance, under risk-based capital thresholds that
  let the OCS take control of a carrier that falls below them. Add the
  statutory deposit locked with the government (for example AIG **$2.6
  million**, Riverstone $3.5 million, Medco $2.5 million) and the catastrophe
  reserve under Chapter 25 and Rule 72 of the Insurance Code, built for the
  hurricane that takes the grid down and sends claims through the roof.
- **The number that ends the myth.** Medical loss ratio: Platino plus
  Medicare Advantage paid roughly **$8.0 billion** in claims on **$9.0 billion**
  in premium, an MLR near **89%**. The whole market paid **$15.49 billion** of
  claims on $17.18 billion, **90.2%**. Plan Vital reached **95.8%** market-wide
  (95.5% among the Core 9). A plan that truly covered only cold medicine would
  run an MLR of maybe 20 to 30% and pocket the rest. Q1 2026 keeps the pattern:
  **83.1%**, $3.76 billion paid on $4.52 billion in three months.

The closing question: CMS is now building the 2027 rate book for Puerto
Rico's municipalities. If Washington tightens those rates even a fraction of
a percent on a market already paying out 90 cents of every dollar and losing
money in the aggregate, where does the system find the money when there are
no efficiencies left to squeeze? You hand a system $440 and ask for a
miracle. It delivers one. What happens next year when you hand it $430?
