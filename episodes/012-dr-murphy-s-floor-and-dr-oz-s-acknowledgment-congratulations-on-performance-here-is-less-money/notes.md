On **August 3, 2026**, Representative Dr. Greg Murphy and 13 other House
Republicans wrote to HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. with a single ask:
a Medicare Advantage benchmark floor for Puerto Rico at **70% of the
national average** for 2028, the level the U.S. Virgin Islands already
receives, using the "similar area" authority in Section 1876(a)(4) of the
Social Security Act. On **September 14, 2026**, CMS answered on the
Secretary's behalf over Dr. Mehmet Oz's signature. This episode reads both
letters, the decade-long administrative record behind them, and the wage
index arithmetic that makes 2028 the year the lawmakers drew the line. No
politics; only the documents and the math.

In this episode:

- **The ask and the answer.** The letter wants a floor because Puerto
  Rico's benchmarks sit about **41% below** the national average and the gap
  has widened every year since 2012. CMS does not grant it. It will "take
  these views into account for the 2028 cycle," calls its approach
  "specific to Puerto Rico and consistent with statutory authorities," and
  points to what it already did for 2027: a **4.4%** adjustment for the
  "propensity of zero claims" and an April 2026 exception letting certain
  dual-eligible special needs plans offer more than one plan in territories
  without Medicare Savings Programs.
- **What a zero claim means.** A population with an unusually high share of
  beneficiaries who generate no claims is not unusually healthy. On a
  low-income island with copays and travel barriers, seniors delay care,
  generate no claim, become invisible to the data, and the data then
  justifies less funding. CMS recognized the distortion; the lawmakers call
  a 4.4% bump a fresh coat of paint on the mailbox of a sinking house.
- **The apartment-building sample.** More than **670,000** Puerto Rico
  seniors, **96%** of the Medicare-eligible population, are in Medicare
  Advantage, the highest rate in the nation. By law the benchmarks are tied
  to the cost of original fee-for-service Medicare in the same area, which
  means the budget for nearly 700,000 people is set from the bills of about
  **29,000**. The letter says that sample is too small to reflect the true
  cost of a functioning program.
- **No safety net under the safety net.** Puerto Rico has no Medicare
  Savings Program, no long-term services and supports, no Supplemental
  Security Income and no standard SNAP. Nearly half of the beneficiaries are
  dual eligible. Medicare Advantage is the de facto safety net, and it is
  funded from a 4% data pool.
- **Twelve years of the same request.** Stakeholders have asked for a floor
  on the record from the CY2016 rate cycle through CY2027. CMS's reason for
  saying no has migrated: not permissible under statute (2016); the
  fee-for-service data is sufficient (2017 to 2018); "continue to analyze"
  (2019); follow the local data and no evidence costs should be higher
  (2020 to 2024); "limited discretion to incorporate exceptions" (2025 to
  2027). The similar-area clause pressed on CMS in 2024 has never been
  addressed in a refusal.
- **Discretion when it wants it.** The record shows CMS finding
  administrative authority without Congress when it chose to: restricting
  the baseline in 2012 to beneficiaries with both Part A and Part B to
  correct Puerto Rico's auto-enrollment skew; classifying **52 of 78**
  municipalities as double-bonus counties for star-rated plans in 2018; the
  zero-claims adjustment; the D-SNP exception. Band-aids are within its
  power; a permanent floor is not.
- **Why 2028.** Puerto Rico's hospital wage index is on a legally locked
  **5%-a-year step-down**. San Juan went from **0.6134** in FY2024 to
  **0.5259** in FY2027, a cumulative **14.26%** drop applied to the 62%
  labor-related share of payment, and the projection runs to roughly
  **0.35** by FY2035. Because the MA benchmarks reprice five years of
  fee-for-service claims with the latest wage index, the 2027 cut lands in
  the 2028 rates. CMS itself wrote in the 2027 announcement that its wage
  index policies "will contribute to declines in the MA rates for Puerto
  Rico." The floor has to exist before the 2028 rates lock.

The closing question: in a system that says it rewards results, the single
market that embraced Medicare Advantage at 96% and delivers the nation's
best star ratings is paid the least. If the best results are reimbursed the
worst, what exactly is the formula measuring? When adherence to a broken
formula overrides the evidence, it is no longer a data error. It is a
failure of government.
