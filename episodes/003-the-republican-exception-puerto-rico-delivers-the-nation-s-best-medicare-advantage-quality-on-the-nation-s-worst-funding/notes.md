Imagine a city's entire grocery budget being set by the receipts of thirty
people in one isolated neighborhood. That statistical absurdity is real — it's
how a hidden federal formula sets Medicare Advantage funding for 676,000
American citizens in Puerto Rico. This episode follows the paper trail of how
a technical grievance became a bipartisan fix — and how fourteen Republicans
ended up leading the charge.

In this episode:

- **The broken thermometer.** Medicare Advantage covers roughly **96%** of
  Puerto Rico's Medicare-eligible population (vs. about 50% on the mainland) —
  yet the benchmark formula prices the island off the tiny fee-for-service
  remnant that's left, primarily veterans and low utilizers.
- **The missing safety nets.** No Medicare Savings Program, no long-term
  services and supports, a capped Medicaid allotment with a **lower federal
  match than territorial peers** — and a Medicaid cliff arriving in 2027,
  while nearly half of the island's MA beneficiaries are dual-eligible,
  low-income seniors.
- **The campaign.** Twenty-six letters to HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
  and CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz; the Puerto Rico Federal Affairs
  Administration aligned with the island's Healthcare Coalition; a deliberate
  strategy to frame an administrative fix — the **0.70 benchmark floor for
  2028** — that bypasses legislative gridlock entirely.
- **The Republican exception.** The August 3, 2026 letter: **14 GOP House
  members** formally requesting the benchmark floor, led by Rep. Gregory
  Murphy of North Carolina — a physician and co-chair of the House GOP
  Doctors Caucus — with pharmacist Rep. Buddy Carter validating the clinical
  reality. Not a favor to insurers: a doctor reading the math and concluding
  the system is being starved.

The closing question: what other outdated algorithms are quietly running in
the background of government — dictating funding for education,
infrastructure, healthcare — detached from the people they're supposed to
serve? Who calibrated the machine, and what is it actually measuring?
